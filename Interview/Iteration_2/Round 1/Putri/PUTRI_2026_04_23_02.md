| **Question** | **Answer** |
|--------------|------------|
| Do you know that the UQ Student Hub on Discord exists? | No, I didn’t know it existed. |
| What do you think the kind of forums or services there would provide? | Probably links to mental health experts, advice websites, and general support resources. |
| When you imagine a “Support” button inside Blackboard, what do you expect it to do or show? | It should provide different options depending on the situation. For example, academic support for stress related to studies, or different help if it’s personal or external problems. It should guide users to different types of support after clicking. |
| Does it matter if the support is from a trained professional or a fellow student? | I’d be happy talking to someone with similar experiences. They don’t have to be a professional as long as they can relate and give useful advice. |
| Why do you think a peer is better at helping? | Professionals can feel too clinical. Sometimes I just want to casually talk about my problems. Someone with similar experiences can relate better and give more practical advice. |
| How would you feel reading a community forum where peers share similar struggles? | I might read it, but I’d be less likely to post. I prefer one-on-one conversations rather than public discussions. |
| If support required zero effort (no booking), would you talk to someone or cope alone? | I’d be much more likely to seek help. Making bookings is a barrier, so if I could just show up and talk, I’d actually do it. |
| What is the maximum time you would wait for a response before going back to your usual coping habits? | About a week. At uni, a week makes a big difference. If there’s no response by then, I’d probably stop seeking help. |
| What would an ideal effortless support experience look like? | A casual, low-pressure space that’s open at convenient times. Somewhere you can just hang out or talk to people, preferably those with similar experiences. It shouldn’t feel like a clinic or psychologist’s office—more like a relaxed, comfortable environment. |
