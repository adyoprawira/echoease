### Section 1: Integrated Access (Blackboard & Discord)

| **Questions** | **Responses** |
|---------------|----------------|
| Do you know that the UQ Student Hub on Discord exists? | Yes, I know it exists. However, I do not know if it is actively managed or if students use it regularly. It feels a bit underpromoted compared to other UQ platforms. |
| What do you think if we were to create a mental care forum within the UQ Student Hub on Discord? | I think that would be a good idea because it would feel more casual and approachable than official websites. Students might feel more comfortable opening up there, especially if it is anonymous and properly moderated. |
| Would you be more likely to use it as someone who reads others' stories or as an active participant posting your own? Why? | I would probably use it more as someone reading other stories first. It feels safer to observe first, and seeing others with similar struggles might make me feel less alone. |
| Let’s say you are having a problem mentally; when you imagine a “Support” button directly inside your UQ Blackboard, what do you expect it to do or show? | I would expect instant access to help, such as resources, peer support chat, counselling bookings, and emergency contacts. It should be simple, quick, and easy to use. |

### Section 2: Community vs. Automation

| **Questions** | **Responses** |
|---------------|----------------|
| Does it matter to you if the person on the other end is a trained professional or a fellow student who has been through similar units? | Yes, it depends on the issue. For serious mental health matters, I would prefer a professional. For study stress or university pressure, I would feel more comfortable talking to another student. |
| Why do you think a trained professional or peer is better at catering to your problems or answering you? | A professional has proper training and can give safer advice. A fellow student can relate better because they understand the real pressure of assignments, exams, and university life. |
| If you are reading on a community forum that fellow peers are in the same situation as you, how would you feel? | I would feel relieved because it reminds me that I am not the only one struggling. It would make my problems feel more normal and less overwhelming. |

### Section 3: Effortless Intervention (Zero-Friction)

| **Questions** | **Responses** |
|---------------|----------------|
| If we provided a support service without requiring you to make a booking (zero effort), would you still prefer to cope with your problem by yourself, or would you talk through it with someone? | I would be more likely to talk with someone if there was no booking required. Usually the biggest barrier is the effort, not the willingness to get help. |
| What is the maximum amount of time you would be willing to wait for a response before you went back to your usual coping habits? | Probably around 10 to 15 minutes. If it takes longer, I would most likely go back to dealing with it myself. |
| What would an ideal "effortless" support experience look like for you? | One click from Blackboard, instant chat access, anonymous options, and quick guidance without long forms. It should feel as easy as messaging someone. |
