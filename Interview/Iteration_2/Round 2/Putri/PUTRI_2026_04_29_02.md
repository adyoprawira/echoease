## Interview 2

**Recording link**: [Listen to Interview 2](https://uq.sharepoint.com/:u:/r/teams/Section_7620_22283/Shared%20Documents/54_Richard/Iteration%202_Interview_SecondRound_Putri/Interview%202.mp3?csf=1&web=1&e=pbqqCu)

| **Question** | **Answer** |
|--------------|------------|
| If there was a button called "Support and Well-being" on your Blackboard dashboard, where would you expect it to be? | On the left-hand side with the tools and courses, or as a pop-up notification that stays visible as you navigate different pages. |
| What would you expect that button to do? | Provide a live chat for fast solutions, and a compilation of frequently asked questions for easy reference. |
| Would accessing well-being support on Blackboard feel natural or unexpected? | A bit unexpected, as I usually associate Blackboard with accessing academic resources. |
| If you saw options for community stories, chat, and emergency resources, what would you think the page is for? | Support and well-being, as all three options fall under that single category. |
| Which of those three options would you pick first and why? | Chat with someone, because I prefer fast solutions. Community stories would be my second choice to see if others have already answered my questions. |
| How comfortable would you feel reading anonymous posts about student struggles? | Generally comfortable if it's relatable, though I'd wonder about the extent of privacy for the people sharing. |
| Would you prefer a "read-only" anonymous community or the ability to reply? | Reply and interact, because both parties can benefit from the shared interaction. |
| Do you think seeing other students' experiences would make you feel less alone? | Definitely. Sometimes we just need a space to share, so it would help a lot. |
| How easy or difficult would it be to write your own anonymous post? | Quite a struggle if I'm currently in it, but I would really like to share stories about things I have already overcome. |
| Would having topic tags (stress, sleep, etc.) help you express yourself? | Yes, I think that would be helpful for posting stories. |
| How important is the option to post anonymously? | Highly important. I don't want people who know me to recognize my story or for it to be widespread in a scary way. |
| In what situations would you actually consider posting? | After a stressful event when my mind is in a better state and I can handle sharing or asking for opinions. |
| How likely are you to use a private chat with a peer or counselor with no booking required? | I would love it. I don't like the booking process and prefer instant, low-effort solutions. |
| Would a peer mentor feel less intimidating than a formal appointment? | Yes. It feels like chatting with a friend who is relatable and open, whereas formal counseling has more "procedure" and costs. |
| What would make you feel safe or unsafe about a chat feature? | Privacy terms. I'd need reassurance on how they manage and keep my information as private as possible. |
| Would a chat option be helpful late at night? | 50-50. Sometimes I just want peace and quiet rather than interacting with people. |
| Where would you expect to find a clearly labeled emergency resources section? | On the left side, available everywhere, but designed so it isn't "abused" or jammed by people using it for fun. |
| What wording would make emergency resources feel trustworthy and accessible? | Something less formal like "Need Help?" since "Emergency" feels like a very big, formal word that might make me reluctant to use it. |
| If you were in a very stressed situation, what would help you quickly recognize urgent help? | A pop-up feature. |
| Would you trust a university platform to direct you to the right emergency contacts? | Yes, I trust the university would reach out to emergency contacts as fast as they can. |
