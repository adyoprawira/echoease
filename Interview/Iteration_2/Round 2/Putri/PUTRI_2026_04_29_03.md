## Interview 3

**Recording link**: [Listen to Interview 3](https://uq.sharepoint.com/:u:/r/teams/Section_7620_22283/Shared%20Documents/54_Richard/Iteration%202_Interview_SecondRound_Putri/Interview%203.mp3?csf=1&web=1&e=ONuUEh)

| **Question** | **Answer** |
|--------------|------------|
| If there was a button called "Support and Well-being" on your Blackboard dashboard, where would you expect it to be? | On the left side with the courses; an obvious button in that left section of the webpage. |
| What would you expect that button to do? | Direct me to counseling services or a live chat feature where I can talk to UQ-trained counseling students. |
| Would accessing well-being support on Blackboard feel natural or unexpected? | A bit unexpected because I am used to Blackboard being for course content and assignments; it would take time to adjust. |
| If you saw options for community stories, chat, and emergency resources, what would you think the page is for? | A page for students' mental health support. |
| Which of those three options would you pick first and why? | Read community stories. I want to know if others are going through the same thing and see if their coping mechanisms might work for me. |
| How comfortable would you feel reading anonymous posts about student struggles? | 50/50. It makes me feel less alone, but I'd limit my time there so the negative experiences don't create a stressful atmosphere for me. |
| Would you prefer a "read-only" anonymous community or the ability to reply? | I would prefer being able to reply and interact, as long as every interaction remains anonymous. |
| Do you think seeing other students' experiences would make you feel less alone? | Yes, definitely, though consuming too much might start to affect me negatively. |
| How easy or difficult would it be to write your own anonymous post? | Easy, because it is anonymous and nobody knows who I am. Receiving support from the community would make me feel much better. |
| Would having topic tags (stress, sleep, etc.) help you express yourself? | Yes. It helps me categorize my feelings and makes it easier to search for others who have spoken about the same topics. |
| How important is the option to post anonymously? | Very important. I don't think I would actually post there if it wasn't anonymous. |
| In what situations would you actually consider posting? | In situations where I need opinions or perspectives from people outside of my close friend group. |
| How likely are you to use a private chat with a peer or counselor with no booking required? | I would love to try it. Booking counseling can be tedious and demotivating, so a "one-click" connection to an expert is nice. |
| Would a peer mentor feel less intimidating than a formal appointment? | Yes, definitely. |
| What would make you feel safe or unsafe about a chat feature? | I would feel unsafe if it wasn't anonymous or if I didn't trust the credibility and qualifications of the person assisting me. |
| Would a chat option be helpful late at night? | It could be helpful, but it wouldn't be the first thing to cross my mind. |
| Where would you expect to find a clearly labeled emergency resources section? | At the top of the screen. |
| What wording would make emergency resources feel trustworthy? | "Emergency Resources" is fine. It sounds formal and makes me feel that people with credibility are running the service. |
| What would help you quickly recognize urgent help in a stressful situation? | Obvious colors, clear labels, and being able to access the help in less than three clicks. |
| Would you trust a university platform to direct you to the right emergency contacts? | Yes. I trust they would understand I am a student and treat me with appropriate expectations for someone needing help. |
