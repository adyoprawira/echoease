## Interview 1

**Recording link**: [Listen to Interview 1](https://uq.sharepoint.com/:v:/r/teams/Section_7620_22283/Shared%20Documents/54_Richard/Iteration%202_Interview_SecondRound_Putri/Interview%201.mp4?csf=1&web=1&e=a7iZUE)

| **Question** | **Answer** |
|--------------|------------|
| If there was a button called "Support and Well-being" on your Blackboard dashboard, where would you expect it to be? | On the left-hand side with all the other resources. |
| What would you expect that button to do? | Provide emergency contacts or places students can refer to when they are struggling. |
| Would accessing well-being support on Blackboard feel natural or unexpected? | A bit unexpected, because Blackboard is usually just for assignments, not for finding support. |
| If you saw options for community stories, chat, and emergency resources, what would you think the page is for? | A safe community space for students to interact and explore. |
| Which of those three options would you pick first and why? | Community stories. It’s a comfort to know if what I'm experiencing is shared by others and that I'm not alone. |
| How comfortable would you feel reading anonymous posts about stress, exams, or finances? | Overall comfortable; I want to see if my experience is shared so I don't feel less alone. |
| Would you prefer a "read-only" community or the ability to reply? | I'd prefer to reply to things I resonate with to tell others they aren't alone. |
| How easy or difficult would it be to write your own anonymous post? | It depends on the issue and how widespread it is among other students. |
| Would having topic tags (stress, sleep, etc.) help you express yourself? | Yes, especially for common topics like stress where people can share helpful tips. |
| How important is the option to post anonymously? | Very important. These topics are personal and private, so anonymity is highly appreciated. |
| In what situations would you actually consider posting? | When feeling really lonely, "in a lost cause," or just needing a new space to express feelings. |
| How likely are you to use a private chat with a peer or counselor with no booking required? | Pretty moderate. I’d consider it if I were really desperate or wanted to talk to someone new. |
| Would a peer mentor feel less intimidating than a formal appointment? | Maybe. It depends on the day, the topic, and the severity of the issue. |
| What would make you feel safe or unsafe about a chat feature? | Privacy regarding how my information and personal details are being shared or used. |
| Would a chat option be helpful late at night or outside office hours? | Yes, if the situation is desperate enough. |
| Where would you expect to find a clearly labeled emergency resources section? | As a feature that constantly "pops up" or stays in the corner (like a chatbot) regardless of scrolling. |
| What wording would make emergency resources feel trustworthy? | Phrases that emphasize support like "We are here for you," rather than commercial or formal language. |
| Would you trust a university platform to direct you to the right emergency contacts? | Yes, especially at UQ because it's a large, established university with reliable resources. |
