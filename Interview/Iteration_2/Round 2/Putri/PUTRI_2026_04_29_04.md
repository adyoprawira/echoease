## Interview 4

**Recording link**: [Listen to Interview 4](https://uq.sharepoint.com/:v:/r/teams/Section_7620_22283/Shared%20Documents/54_Richard/Iteration%202_Interview_SecondRound_Putri/Interview%204.mp4?csf=1&web=1&e=q51vdu)

| **Question** | **Answer** |
|--------------|------------|
| If there was a button called "Support and Well-being" on your Blackboard dashboard, where would you expect it to be? | In the header section or the left-hand side navigation for easy access. |
| What would you expect that button to do? | Help me connect with authorized personnel who can provide mental health support or guidelines. |
| Would accessing well-being support on Blackboard feel natural or unexpected? | 50/50. It makes sense to have it there, but it might feel more natural on the UQ Student Portal. |
| If you saw options for community stories, chat, and emergency resources, what would you think the page is for? | Community stories for feedback on experiences, chat for direct connections/chatbots for help, and emergency resources for locating nearby health clinics. |
| Which of those three options would you pick first and why? | Emergency resources, because I prefer physical help. However, chat would be my first choice if it were late at night and I needed urgent help. |
| How clear does the layout of these three options feel to you? | As a first-timer with no background in this, the layout feels clear enough. |
| How comfortable would you feel reading anonymous student posts about stress or financial problems? | Comfortable, as long as the stories aren't too extreme. If they are very heavy, I might just feel pity and overthink how to help them. |
| Would you prefer a "read-only" community or the ability to reply? | I'd prefer to be able to reply, as long as my profile and identity remain completely anonymous. |
| Do you think seeing other students' experiences would make you feel less alone? | Yes, it helps to know others are struggling too, but it might also make me feel sad for them. |
| How easy or difficult would it be to share your own story anonymously? | Easy. Since no one knows me, I can speak freely and share what I want. |
| Would having topic tags (stress, sleep, etc.) help you express yourself? | Yes, that would be a great feature to have. |
| In what situations would you actually consider posting something? | When I experience something unbearable and receive help; I’d want to share that success to help others in the same boat. |
| How likely are you to use a private chat with a peer or counselor with no booking required? | Once or twice a year, likely during the "crucial time" of final exam season. |
| Would a peer mentor feel less intimidating than a formal appointment? | I'm not sure. I might prefer formal counseling because they are authorized experts, but peers are good for immediate, relatable help. |
| What would make you feel safe or unsafe about a chat feature? | Anonymity is the main factor. I need to know my privacy is kept secret and not widespread. |
| Would a chat option be helpful late at night? | Yes, it provides immediate support when friends or personal support systems might be unavailable. |
| Where would you expect to find a clearly labeled emergency resources section? | In the header section, perhaps as a prominent button on the right. |
| What wording would make emergency resources feel trustworthy? | "Emergency Resources" is good, or something direct like "Click here for emergency." |
| What would help you quickly recognize urgent help in a stressful situation? | A filterable list or table where I can find resources by location (like St. Lucia) with buttons that direct me to the specific resource page. |
| Would you trust a university platform to direct you to the right emergency contacts? | Yes. I believe the resources provided by the university are authorized, verified, and endorsed. |
