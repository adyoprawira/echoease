## Interview 2
 **Recording link**: [Listen to Interview 2](https://uq.sharepoint.com/:u:/r/teams/Section_7620_22283/Shared%20Documents/54_Richard/Iteration%202_Interview_Nabiha%20Farisha/Round%202/Iteration%202_Round%202_Interview%202_Farisha.m4a?csf=1&web=1&e=1RI1g7)

| **Questions** | **Responses** |
|---------------|---------------|
| Imagine you are on your usual Blackboard dashboard. If there was a button called Support & Well‑being, where would you expect it to be located? | I would expect it to be on the navigation bar, either at the top or on the side, because that’s how Blackboard usually works. A pop‑up could also help make students aware of the feature. |
|  If you saw a Support & Well‑being button on Blackboard, what would you expect it to do? | I would expect it to let students talk to someone, maybe a therapist, or show information about wellbeing support. |
| Would you naturally think of Blackboard as a place to access wellbeing support, or would that feel unexpected? | It would feel a bit unexpected, but also cool. Students open Blackboard every day, so it would make support easier to access immediately. |
| Imagine you click a support button and land on a page with three options: Community Stories, Chat with Someone, and Emergency Resources. What would you think this page is for?  | Community Stories would let students read others’ experiences. Chat with Someone would connect to a therapist or available friend. Emergency Resources would provide helplines or emergency contacts. | 
|  Which of those three options would you be most likely to click first, and why? | Community Stories, to see if others have similar experiences and feel less alone. |
| Does that kind of layout feel clear to you, or would you expect something different? | Yes, it’s clear and well‑planned. |
| Imagine there’s an anonymous community where students share short posts about stress, loneliness, exams, or finance. How comfortable would you feel reading posts like that? | Comfortable; interested in others’ stories; helps build awareness and understanding. |
| Would a read‑only anonymous community feel safe and helpful, or would you prefer being able to reply or interact? | Prefer interaction, as long as it’s supportive and not harmful. |
| Do you think seeing other students’ experiences would make you feel less alone? | Yes, it helps knowing others are in similar situations. |
| Imagine you could write an anonymous post about something you’re struggling with. How easy or difficult would it be to share something in that space? | Easy, because anonymity makes expressing feelings safer. | 
| If you could choose up to three topic tags (Stress, Anxiety, Sleep, Relationships), would that help you express what you’re going through? | Yes — stress, anxiety, sleep, and relationships all apply to me. |
| How important is the option to post anonymously for you? | Very important; I don’t want people knowing my name. | 
| In what situation would you actually consider posting something? | When feeling helpless, stressed, or unsure what to do. |
| Imagine there’s a private chat where you can talk to a peer mentor or counsellor without booking anything. How likely are you to use something like that? | Very likely; booking is stressful and not guaranteed. |
| Would chatting with a peer mentor feel less intimidating than booking a formal counselling appointment? | Yes; booking processes make me anxious. | 
| What would make you feel safe or unsafe about using a chat feature for support? | Unsafe if chats could leak; platform must have strong security. |
| If you were stressed late at night, would a chat option feel helpful? | Yes, if responses are quick; not helpful if replies are slow. |
| Imagine there’s a clearly labelled Emergency Resources section inside a wellbeing platform. Where would you expect to find it? | At the bottom, where emergency contacts usually are. |
| What wording would make emergency resources feel clear and trustworthy to you? | Reassuring phrases like “You are not alone,” plus 24/7 helplines. |
| If you were in a stressful situation, what would help you quickly recognise where urgent help is located? | Emergency contacts placed at the bottom navigation or top “About” section. |
