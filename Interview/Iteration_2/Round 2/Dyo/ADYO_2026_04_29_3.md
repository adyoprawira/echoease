## Interview 3

**Recording link**: [Listen to Interview 3](https://uq.sharepoint.com/:u:/r/teams/Section_7620_22283/Shared%20Documents/54_Richard/Iteration%202_Round%202_Interview%203_Adyo%20Arkan%20Prawira.m4a?csf=1&web=1&e=6OLqax)

| Questions | Responses |
|----------|-----------|
| Imagine you’re on your usual Blackboard dashboard. If there was a button called Support & Well-being, where would you expect it to be located? | Somewhere accessible in the main navigation. |
| If you saw a Support & Well-being button on Blackboard, what would you expect it to do? | Provide support information and helpful resources. |
| Would you naturally think of Blackboard as a place to access wellbeing support, or would that feel unexpected? | It would feel somewhat unexpected since it’s mainly for academics. |
| Imagine you click a support button and land on a page with three options: Read Community Stories, Chat with Someone, and Emergency Resources. What would you think this page is for? | Different pathways to get support depending on the situation. |
| Which of those three options would you be most likely to click first, and why? | Community stories, to understand others’ experiences first. |
| Does that kind of layout feel clear to you, or would you expect something different? | Yes, it feels clear. |
| Imagine there’s an anonymous community where students share short posts about stress, loneliness, exams, or finances. How comfortable would you feel reading posts like that? | Moderately comfortable, depending on how personal the posts are. |
| Would a read-only anonymous community feel safe and helpful, or would you prefer being able to reply or interact? | Read-only feels safer. |
| Do you think seeing other students’ experiences would make you feel less alone, or would it not affect you much? | Yes, it would help me feel less alone. |
| Imagine you could write an anonymous post about something you’re struggling with. How easy or difficult do you think it would be to share something in that space? | Still somewhat difficult, but easier than non-anonymous posting. |
| If you could choose up to three topic tags (like Stress, Anxiety, Sleep, Relationships), would that help you express what you’re going through? | Yes, it helps structure thoughts. |
| How important is the option to post anonymously for you? In what situations would you actually consider posting something? | Very important. I’d post when feeling overwhelmed or needing support. |
| Imagine there’s a private chat where you can talk to a peer mentor or counsellor without booking anything. How likely are you to use something like that? | Likely, because it’s quick and accessible. |
| Would chatting with a peer mentor feel less intimidating than booking a formal counselling appointment? What would make you feel safe or unsafe about using a chat feature for support? | Yes, less intimidating. Safe if private and controlled, unsafe if privacy isn’t guaranteed. |
| If you were stressed late at night, would a chat option feel helpful? | Yes. |
| Imagine there’s a clearly labelled Emergency Resources section inside a wellbeing platform. Where would you expect to find it? | Within the support section, clearly visible. |
| What wording would make emergency resources feel clear and trustworthy to you? | Clear references to emergency and health support. |
| If you were in a stressful situation, what would help you quickly recognise where urgent help is located? | Visual cues like strong colors or prominent placement. |
| Would you trust a university platform to direct you to the right emergency contacts? | Yes. |
