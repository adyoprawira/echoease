## Interview 1

**Recording link**: [Listen to Interview 1](https://uq.sharepoint.com/:u:/r/teams/Section_7620_22283/Shared%20Documents/54_Richard/Iteration%202_Interview%201_Adyo%20Arkan%20Prawira.m4a?csf=1&web=1&e=7BkyBF)

| Questions | Responses |
|----------|-----------|
| Do you know that the UQ student hub on Discord exists? | Yes, of course. |
| What do you think if we were to create a mental care forum within the UQ student hub? | I think it's a good idea because Discord feels more informal and approachable compared to official platforms. People might open up more there, but it only works well if there's proper moderation. |
| Would you be more likely to use it as a reader or an active participant? | I'd start as a reader because it feels safer. I'd only start posting if the environment feels supportive and non-judgmental. |
| Does it matter if the person on the other end is a professional or a student? | Students have more relatable experiences with courses or assignments, so their advice feels more practical. |
| If you're reading on a community forum that fellow peers are in the same situation as you, how would you feel? | I would feel relieved because it makes the problem feel normal and less isolating. If everyone is too negative, it could also make things feel worse. |
| If we provided a support service without requiring a booking, would you prefer to cope alone or talk to someone? | I'm more likely to talk to someone since the barrier is lower. |
| What is the maximum amount of time you would be willing to wait for a response? | Ideally under 5 minutes. Around 15 minutes after that, I'd probably stop waiting. |
| What would an ideal effortless support experience look like for you? | Near-instant reply, option to stay anonymous, no long forms, and the ability to choose between peer or professional support. It should feel like sending a message, not accessing a formal service. |
