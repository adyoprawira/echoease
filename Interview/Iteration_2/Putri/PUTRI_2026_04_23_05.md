| **Question** | **Answer** |
|--------------|------------|
| Do you know that the UQ Student Hub exists? | Yes, I’ve visited the webpage occasionally, but not very often. |
| What do you think about creating a mental health forum within the UQ Student Hub? | It would be very helpful. A forum allows people to share thoughts without the pressure of attending formal sessions like with a psychologist. It could be a less daunting first step to seek help. |
| Would you be more likely to read others’ stories or actively post your own? | I would mainly read others’ stories. It could help me understand how to handle similar struggles if I face them in the future. |
| When you imagine a “Support” button inside Blackboard, what do you expect it to do or show? | It should provide initial reassurance and suggest ways to process or overcome problems in a healthier way. |
| Does it matter if the support is from a trained professional or a fellow student? | I don’t mind either. Even trained students are acceptable, as they are capable and also gaining experience while helping others. |
| How would you feel reading a community forum where peers share similar struggles? | It would make me feel less alone and reassured that others are going through similar experiences. |
| If support required zero effort (no booking), would you talk to someone or cope alone? | I would prefer a walk-in session to talk to someone directly, as it creates a human connection rather than interacting through a screen. |
| What is the maximum time you would wait for a response before going back to your usual coping habits? | About a week, considering current university services already have waiting times. |
| What would an ideal effortless support experience look like? | A walk-in system where you can get help immediately when needed, without waiting weeks or making bookings. Immediate, in-person support is most ideal. |
