## Interview 2
 **Recording link**: [Listen to Interview 1]

| **Questions** | **Responses** |
|---------------|---------------|
|Do you know that UQ Student Hub on Discord exists?| No I do not know that.|
|What do you think of the kind of forums the channel provides there? | I think it would be beneficial for people because it feels less serious and less intimidating. Since it’s like social media, people are used to messaging friends there, so it feels like a good communication channel for those who want to reach out without it feeling too formal.|
| Let’s say you are having a problem mentally, when you imagine a “Support” button directly inside your UQ Blackboard, what do you expect it to do or show?| Probably something like a Microsoft Forms link where you can submit your concerns. Basically, a form to reach out for support. |
|Does it matter to you if the person on the other end is a trained professional or a fellow student who has been through similar units?|Both have benefits. A student could be helpful if they’ve been through similar experiences and can give genuine advice.|
Why do you think a fellow peers is better in catering to your problems/ answering you? |Maybe the student, because their responses feel more individual and less “manufactured.” Professionals might give the same standard answers, while students can offer something more personal.|
|If you are reading on the community forum that fellow peers are having the same situation as you, how would you feel?| It would be reassuring to know others have gone through the same thing. It might also make me more inclined to ask for help, especially if I see that the first step isn’t directly going to a professional.| 
|If we provide this service without requiring you to make a booking (zero effort), do you still want to cope with your problem by yourself or would you talk through it with someone?|Having something available immediately would be helpful. When you’re struggling, it’s often in the moment. Booking an appointment can cause anxiety or make you lose the motivation to reach out. Instant access would make more people use the service.|
| What is the maximum amount of time you would be willing to wait for a response before you went back to your usual coping habits?| It depends on the situation, but probably around 5–10 minutes. About the same time you’d expect a good friend to reply.|
|What would an ideal effortless support experience look like for you?|Easily accessible through Blackboard, anonymous, relaxed rather than formal, and with someone around my age who has gone through similar things. Being able to talk from your own space makes it easier to be open compared to a formal therapist office.|
