## Hypothesis 1 : Students will use services more if the process is intuitive and centralized.

**HYPOTHESIS**
We believed that students will use services more if the process is intuitive and centralized.

**OBSERVATIONS**
We observed that across interviews, students showed mixed awareness of services, but even when aware, they rarely use them. Some students explicitly mentioned confusion when navigating university support systems, being referred to multiple places, and experiencing delays. Accessibility is also limited by office hours, making services less usable in real-time situations.

**LEARNING AND INSIGHTS**
From that we learnt that simply making services available or even known is not sufficient to drive usage. Students tend to disengage if the process feels unclear, slow, or not directly relevant to their needs. Convenience and immediacy play a significant role in decision-making.

**DECISIONS AND ACTION** 
Therefore, we will partially accept this hypothesis and further investigate how to simplify access, improve clarity, and reduce friction in the service journey.



## Hypothesis 2 : Anonymity and low-effort entry points will increase service uptake compared to formal appointments.

**HYPOTHESIS**
We believed that anonymity and low-effort entry points will increase service uptake compared to formal appointments.

**OBSERVATIONS**
We observed that many students expressed a preference for privacy and anonymity when dealing with personal issues. Across interviews, students consistently chose low-effort coping strategies over formal services. Some indicated they would feel more comfortable starting with something simple (e.g., online tools) before committing to formal help.

**LEARNING AND INSIGHTS**
From that we learnt that anonymity reduces psychological barriers, while low-effort entry points align better with students’ natural behaviour. Formal systems may feel intimidating, time-consuming, or unnecessary unless the situation becomes severe.

**DECISIONS AND ACTION** 
Therefore, we will accept this hypothesis and focus on designing anonymous, low-effort entry points that can act as a bridge to more structured support.



## Hypothesis 3 : Part-time working students avoid financial aid because it isn't "guided" or easy to access.

**HYPOTHESIS**
We believed that part-time working students avoid financial aid because it isn't "guided" or easy to access.

**OBSERVATIONS**
We observed that students facing financial challenges tend to rely on part-time work, budgeting, or family support. Across interviews, financial aid services were rarely mentioned or considered. Some students lacked awareness, while others preferred independence or felt that aid should be reserved for those in greater need.

**LEARNING AND INSIGHTS**
From that we learnt that avoidance of financial aid is not solely due to accessibility issues. It is also influenced by mindset (self-reliance, perceived fairness), lack of visibility, and limited understanding of available options.

**DECISIONS AND ACTION** 
Therefore, we will partially accept this hypothesis and further explore both structural (access/guidance) and psychological (beliefs/perceptions) barriers.



## Hypothesis 4 : Students prefer self-coping unless an intervention is "extremely low-effort."

**HYPOTHESIS**
We believed that students prefer self-coping unless an intervention is "extremely low-effort".

**OBSERVATIONS**
We observed that all students reported using self-coping strategies such as entertainment, rest, exercise, or personal reflection. Seeking help from others (friends/family) happens occasionally, while formal services are typically not considered unless stress becomes overwhelming.

**LEARNING AND INSIGHTS**
From that we learnt that self-coping is the dominant and default behaviour. Students prioritise solutions that are immediate, familiar, and require minimal effort. External interventions are only considered when internal coping is no longer sufficient.

**DECISIONS AND ACTION** 
Therefore, we will accept this hypothesis and design solutions that are extremely low-effort and integrate naturally into students’ existing coping habits.
