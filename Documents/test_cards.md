## Hypothesis 1: Intuitive & Centralized Services

**HYPOTHESIS**
We believe that students will use services more if the process is intuitive and centralized.

**TEST**
To verify that we will ask 5 students to find the "Financial Aid" page on the uni site while we observe.

**METRIC**
And measure time taken (seconds) and observe frustration signals.

**CRITERIA**
We are right if the task is completed in under 60 seconds without the student getting lost.

---

## Hypothesis 2: Anonymity vs. Formal Entry

**HYPOTHESIS**
We believe that anonymity and low-effort entry points will increase service uptake compared to formal appointments.

**TEST**
To verify that we will show 10 students two options: A) "Talk to a counselor" or B) "Take an anonymous 2-minute stress quiz."

**METRIC**
And measure the preference ratio between the two options.

**CRITERIA**
We are right if >80% of students choose the anonymous quiz.

---

## Hypothesis 3: Financial Aid Accessibility for Workers

**HYPOTHESIS**
We believe that part-time working students avoid financial aid because it isn’t guided or easy to access.

**TEST**
To verify that we will interview 3 part-time working students and ask: “What’s the one thing stopping you from asking the uni for money?”

**METRIC**
And measure the frequency of specific reasons (reason tally).

**CRITERIA**
We are right if “too much effort” or “don’t know how” are the top-cited reasons.

---

## Hypothesis 4: Self-Coping vs. Low-Effort Intervention

**HYPOTHESIS**
We believe that students prefer self-coping unless the intervention is extremely low-effort.

**TEST**
To verify that we will send a simple check-in text to 5 people: “How’s your stress? Want a link to the uni support page?”

**METRIC**
And measure the response rate and the specific type of responses received.

**CRITERIA**
We are right if they decline formal help in favor of self-coping (e.g., “I’ll just go for a walk” or “I’ll study more”).
