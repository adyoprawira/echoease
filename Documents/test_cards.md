## Hypothesis 1: Intuitive & Centralized Services

**HYPOTHESIS**
We believe that students will use services more if the process is intuitive and centralized.

**TEST**
To confirm that, we will interview university students.

**METRIC**
And measure the percentage of students who report that "formality" or "office hours" are bigger barriers than actually finding the information.

**CRITERIA**
We are right if 70% of interviewees feel that centralized services are too "professional" or "formal" for personal stress.

**INTERVIEW QUESTIONS**
1. "How do you manage your time juggling between school and your personal matters?"
2. "When you're struggling with stress at 2:00 AM, where do you turn first? Does school support help you at that hour?"

## Hypothesis 2: Anonymity and Low-Effort Entry Points

**HYPOTHESIS**
We believe that anonymity and low-effort entry points will increase service uptake compared to formal appointments.

**TEST**
To confirm that, we will interview university students.

**METRIC**
And measure the percentage of students who value "human conversation" over "automated tools" like quizzes, even if both are anonymous.

**CRITERIA**
We are right if 60% of students state they prefer a human connection (like a chat) over an automated quiz or survey.

**INTERVIEW QUESTIONS**
1. "What would make a support service feel “safe” enough for you to open up on the first try?"
2. "Do you prefer talking to a counsellor or taking an online quiz? Why?"

## Hypothesis 3: Financial Aid Accessibility for Workers

**HYPOTHESIS**
We believe that part-time working students avoid financial aid because it isn’t "guided" or easy to access.

**TEST**
To confirm that, we will interview university students (specifically those working part-time).

**METRIC**
And measure the percentage of students who view seeking financial aid as a sign of "neediness" rather than a resource they've earned.

**CRITERIA**
We are right if 75% of working students say they prefer "just working more hours" over asking the university for financial support.

**INTERVIEW QUESTIONS**
1. "Have you experienced any financial problems? How did you handle them?"
2. "On a scale of 1–10, how stressed are you about your debt?"
3. "What’s the one thing stopping you from asking the university for financial help?"

## Hypothesis 4: Self-Coping vs. Low-Effort Intervention

**HYPOTHESIS**
We believe that students prefer self-coping unless an intervention is "extremely low-effort."

**TEST**
To confirm that, we will interview university students.

**METRIC**
And measure the percentage of students who feel that talking to a counselor feels like "extra labor" compared to their usual coping habits.

**CRITERIA**
We are right if 80% of students describe their first instinct during stress as a "low-friction" activity (like gaming, gym, or music) rather than professional help.

**INTERVIEW QUESTIONS**
1. "What do you do to get yourself out of stress?"
2. "Is there any point in your life that made you feel like giving up? Why?"
3. "Do you prefer to be alone or talk to someone when you're not in a good mental state?"
4. "Do you feel like you currently have enough support around you?"
