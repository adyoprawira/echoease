## Value Propositions

- **Instant, low-friction wellbeing support**
  - Booking-free chat support reduces barriers to help-seeking
  - Encourages students to seek support during stressful moments  
  - Validated by [Hypothesis 7 – accepted](https://github.com/COMP1100-2026-s1/54_Richard/blob/main/Documents/Iteration%203/test_cards.md#test-card-7-zero-friction-intervention)

- **Integrated wellbeing access inside Blackboard**
  - Centralises support within a familiar university platform
  - Improves visibility and accessibility of wellbeing services  
  - Validated by [Hypothesis 5 – accepted](https://github.com/COMP1100-2026-s1/54_Richard/blob/main/Documents/Iteration%203/test_cards.md#test-card-5-integrated-access-refined-from-centralization)

- **Hybrid peer and professional support model**
  - Peer supporters provide relatable everyday support
  - Serious situations can be escalated to professionals  
  - Validated by [Hypothesis 6 – accepted](https://github.com/COMP1100-2026-s1/54_Richard/blob/main/Documents/Iteration%203/test_cards.md#test-card-6-community-vs-automation-refined-from-anonymity-hypothesis)

- **Anonymous community reassurance**
  - Students can safely read and share experiences without pressure
  - Reduces isolation and normalises support-seeking  
  - Supported by [Hypothesis 6 – nuanced acceptance](https://github.com/COMP1100-2026-s1/54_Richard/blob/main/Documents/Iteration%203/test_cards.md#test-card-6-community-vs-automation-refined-from-anonymity-hypothesis)

- **Fast response system**
  - Reduces disengagement caused by delayed support
  - Encourages continued use instead of self-coping alone  
  - Validated by [Hypothesis 4 – accepted](https://github.com/COMP1100-2026-s1/54_Richard/blob/main/Documents/Iteration%203/test_cards.md#test-card-4-self-coping-vs-low-effort-intervention)

- **Reduced stigma around support-seeking**
  - Frames wellbeing and financial support as practical student assistance
  - Encourages students to seek help earlier rather than avoiding services  
  - Refined from findings in [Hypothesis 4](https://github.com/COMP1100-2026-s1/54_Richard/blob/main/Documents/Iteration%203/test_cards.md#test-card-4-self-coping-vs-low-effort-intervention) and [Hypothesis 7](https://github.com/COMP1100-2026-s1/54_Richard/blob/main/Documents/Iteration%203/test_cards.md#test-card-7-zero-friction-intervention)

## Customer Relationships
- Anonymous and low-pressure first contact  
- Self-service wellbeing tools with optional escalation to human support  
- Peer-to-peer engagement and community-based reassurance  
- Personalised recommendations based on student needs and context  
- Ongoing engagement through reminders, check-ins, and supportive notifications  
- Human-centred interaction that balances automation with meaningful support

# Accepted and Refuted Hypotheses

## Accepted Hypotheses
- Students prefer self-coping strategies unless support is extremely low-friction and easy to access.  
  Supported by [Hypothesis 4 – accepted](https://github.com/COMP1100-2026-s1/54_Richard/blob/main/Documents/Iteration%203/test_cards.md#test-card-4-self-coping-vs-low-effort-intervention)

- Students are more likely to engage with support when it is instant, booking-free, and integrated into platforms they already use.  
  Supported by [Hypothesis 5 – accepted](https://github.com/COMP1100-2026-s1/54_Richard/blob/main/Documents/Iteration%203/test_cards.md#test-card-5-integrated-access-refined-from-centralization) and [Hypothesis 7 – accepted](https://github.com/COMP1100-2026-s1/54_Richard/blob/main/Documents/Iteration%203/test_cards.md#test-card-7-zero-friction-intervention)

- Students prefer relatable peer-based support alongside professional services rather than fully automated systems.  
  Supported by [Hypothesis 6 – accepted](https://github.com/COMP1100-2026-s1/54_Richard/blob/main/Documents/Iteration%203/test_cards.md#test-card-6-community-vs-automation-refined-from-anonymity-hypothesis)

- Delayed responses increase disengagement, making fast response systems important for continued use.  
  Supported by [Hypothesis 4 – accepted](https://github.com/COMP1100-2026-s1/54_Richard/blob/main/Documents/Iteration%203/test_cards.md#test-card-4-self-coping-vs-low-effort-intervention)

---

## Refuted / Refined Hypotheses
- Anonymity alone does not significantly increase service uptake when support relies only on automated quizzes or static resources.  
  Refined through findings in [Hypothesis 6 – nuanced acceptance](https://github.com/COMP1100-2026-s1/54_Richard/blob/main/Documents/Iteration%203/test_cards.md#test-card-6-community-vs-automation-refined-from-anonymity-hypothesis)

- Centralisation alone is insufficient if services still feel administrative or inaccessible during stressful moments.  
  Refined through findings in [Hypothesis 5 – accepted](https://github.com/COMP1100-2026-s1/54_Richard/blob/main/Documents/Iteration%203/test_cards.md#test-card-5-integrated-access-refined-from-centralization)

- Financial aid avoidance is influenced not only by accessibility issues, but also by stigma, guilt, and self-reliance attitudes among students.  
  Refined through findings from [Hypothesis 4](https://github.com/COMP1100-2026-s1/54_Richard/blob/main/Documents/Iteration%203/test_cards.md#test-card-4-self-coping-vs-low-effort-intervention)
