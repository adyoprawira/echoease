## Prototype Feedback and MVP Feature Decisions

### 1. Blackboard Support Button
**Prototype Tested:**  
Paper and digital mockups showing a “Support & Wellbeing” button in the Blackboard sidebar.

**Feedback Received:**  
- Students immediately recognised the button and expected it to lead to wellbeing support.  
- They said it felt “natural” and reduced the effort of searching multiple websites.  
- Several students said they would only use support if it was “right there where I study”.

**MVP Decision:**  
We kept this feature as the main entry point.  
The strong feedback confirmed that integration inside Blackboard removes friction and increases help‑seeking.

---

### 2. Anonymous Forum
**Prototype Tested:**  
Screens showing a feed of anonymous posts, topic tags, and a “Create Post” page.

**Feedback Received:**  
- Students felt safer reading anonymous stories first.  
- Many preferred passive browsing before posting.  
- They said anonymity reduced pressure and made the space feel relatable.

**MVP Decision:**  
We included the anonymous forum as a core feature.  
Feedback validated that students want a low‑pressure, anonymous space before seeking direct help.

---

### 3. Private Chat (No Booking)
**Prototype Tested:**  
Chat interface mockups showing instant messaging with a peer mentor.

**Feedback Received:**  
- Students said chat felt “less intimidating” than counselling.  
- They emphasised that booking forms and long waits made them avoid support.  
- They wanted fast responses, especially at night.

**MVP Decision:**  
We kept the private chat feature and prioritised zero‑booking access.  
Feedback confirmed that instant, casual chat aligns with students’ natural coping habits.

---

### 4. Emergency Resources
**Prototype Tested:**  
Emergency section placement, colour, and wording.

**Feedback Received:**  
- Students expected emergency help to be clearly visible and at the top.  
- They preferred red or high‑contrast colours.  
- They wanted simple, direct wording during stress.

**MVP Decision:**  
We redesigned the emergency section to be more prominent and visually urgent.  
Feedback directly shaped the placement and visual design.
