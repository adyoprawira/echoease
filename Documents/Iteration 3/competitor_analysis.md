# Competitor Analysis

> We plan three feature areas:  
> 1) **Blackboard Support Button**  
> 2) **Anonymous Forum**  
> 3) **Private Chat**

---

## 1) Blackboard Support Button (Integrated Access)

| Competitor | Direct/Indirect | Target Customers | Key Value Proposition | Weaknesses | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Our MVP (UQ Support)** | Direct | UQ students | One‑click wellbeing access inside Blackboard; zero‑friction entry; instant visibility where students already study daily | Dependent on Blackboard’s UI constraints; requires UQ IT approval | Strongest differentiator; validated by 90% preference for Blackboard integration |
| **UQ Counselling & Wellbeing** | Indirect | Enrolled students | Official, trusted, professional support with clinical staff | High friction: booking, forms, office hours; feels “too formal” | Students avoid it for early‑stage stress; used mainly for serious issues |
| **TalkCampus** | Direct | Global university students | 24/7 peer support app with SSO login | Requires separate app download; not embedded in study workflow | Good for after-hours but still adds friction |
| **UQ Student Hub (Discord)** | Indirect | UQ students | Community space for social connection and peer chat | Low awareness (65% didn’t know it existed); not wellbeing‑specific | Students don’t associate it with mental health support |
| **headspace Care** | Indirect | Youth (12–25) | Professional mental health support via mobile app | Feels clinical; requires app download; not integrated with university systems | Useful for serious cases but not for everyday stress |

---

## 2) Anonymous Forum (Community Stories)

| Competitor | Direct/Indirect | Target Customers | Key Value Proposition | Weaknesses | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Our MVP (UQ Support)** | Direct | UQ students | Anonymous, moderated, UQ‑specific stories; passive reading for reassurance; tags for clarity | Requires active moderation; risk of low early engagement | Strong alignment with student preference for passive consumption (85%) |
| **Togetherall** | Direct | Students & employees | Clinically moderated anonymous community with creative “bricks” | Interface feels outdated; less relatable to younger students | Very safe but not UQ‑specific |
| **Reddit (r/uq)** | Indirect | UQ community | Highly active, relatable student discussions | No moderation for wellbeing; risk of toxicity | Students compare anonymous forums to Reddit but want safer space |
| **TalkCampus** | Direct | Global students | Anonymous peer support with 24/7 moderation | Not tailored to UQ context; separate app | Good safety but lacks local relevance |
| **Discord Communities** | Indirect | Students & gamers | Casual peer chat, social support | Not anonymous; not wellbeing‑focused | Students don’t trust it for sensitive topics |

---

## 3) Private Chat (Instant, No‑Booking Support)

| Competitor | Direct/Indirect | Target Customers | Key Value Proposition | Weaknesses | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Our MVP (UQ Support)** | Direct | UQ students | Zero‑booking, instant peer chat; casual “texting” feel; escalation to professionals | Requires enough peer mentors to maintain fast response times | Strongest match to student demand for low‑friction human support |
| **UQ Counselling** | Indirect | Enrolled students | Professional, qualified counsellors | Booking required; long wait times; intimidating | Students avoid unless crisis-level |
| **Wysa** | Indirect | Students seeking AI tools | 24/7 AI chatbot with CBT exercises | Feels impersonal; 60% of students dislike automated tools | Good for privacy but lacks human warmth |
| **headspace Care** | Direct | Youth | Text-based professional support | Can feel clinical; may have wait times | Trusted but not casual enough for everyday stress |
| **TalkCampus** | Direct | Global students | Peer chat + community | Not UQ-specific; separate app | Good for relatability but adds friction |
