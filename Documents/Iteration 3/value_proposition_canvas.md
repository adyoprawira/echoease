# Value Proposition Canvas (Iteration 3)

## Customer Segment: Students experiencing stress, burnout, and difficulty balancing university and personal life

### Customer Jobs
- Balance academic responsibilities with personal life  
- Manage stress without harming academic performance  
- Access support quickly during peak stress moments  
- Maintain emotional stability while handling deadlines and responsibilities  

### Customer Pains
- Delays and friction in accessing university support (booking, wait times)  
- Formal services feel intimidating and overly clinical  
- Lack of support during late-night or urgent stress periods  
- High emotional effort required to seek help  
- Feeling isolated but unwilling to actively reach out  
- Disengagement when support is slow or unresponsive  
- Financial support feels uncomfortable, stigmatized, or “earned through struggle”  

### Customer Gains
- Immediate, low-effort access to support  
- A safe, anonymous, and non-judgmental environment  
- Support that fits naturally into existing student platforms  
- Flexible support (self-help, peer, or professional)  
- Feeling heard quickly, without long delays  
- Reassurance without needing to actively participate  

---

## Value Map 

### Products and Services
- Chat-based instant emotional support system (primary channel)  
- Integration within Blackboard for seamless, one-click access  
- Peer-first support system with escalation to professionals  
- Anonymous, moderated community forum (passive + active use)  
- Real-time / near-instant response infrastructure  
- Lightweight guidance tools (supporting, not replacing human interaction)  

---

### Pain Relievers 

- Eliminates friction by removing booking systems and delays  
  → (Learning: students reject effort, not support)

- Reduces intimidation by replacing formal processes with casual chat  
  → (Learning: professional help feels like “extra labour”)

- Provides immediate or near-instant responses  
  → (Learning: slow response = disengagement)

- Enables after-hours access during peak stress moments  
  → (Learning: stress often occurs outside service hours)

- Embeds support inside Blackboard to remove search effort  
  → (Learning: discoverability, not availability, is the issue)

- Avoids over-reliance on automation  
  → (Learning: students prefer human interaction over tools)

- Allows anonymous, passive engagement  
  → (Learning: students prefer reading before participating)

- Reframes financial support as normal and accessible  
  → (Learning: avoidance driven by stigma, not just access)

---

### Gain Creators 

- Provides support that feels as easy as messaging a friend  
  → (Learning: familiarity increases likelihood of use)

- Integrates into daily academic workflow  
  → (Learning: students engage more within existing platforms)

- Builds trust through peer relatability and shared experience  
  → (Learning: peers provide emotional validation)

- Offers reassurance through passive community consumption  
  → (Learning: many users are observers, not contributors)

- Maintains engagement through fast, responsive interaction  
  → (Learning: delay causes drop-off)

- Supports multiple coping pathways:
  - self-coping (default)
  - peer support (first escalation)
  - professional help (final escalation)  
  → (Learning: support must align with natural coping hierarchy)

---

## Hypothesis Validation 

### Accepted
- Students prefer low-friction, immediate support over formal systems  
- Integration into Blackboard significantly increases accessibility  
- Peer-based interaction is more engaging than automated tools  

### Refuted
- Automation + anonymity alone increases engagement → False  
  → Human interaction is still required  

### Partially Refuted
- Centralization alone solves access → False  
  → Must be embedded into existing workflows  

- Financial aid avoidance is due to accessibility → Incomplete  
  → Driven by stigma, identity, and self-reliance  

---

## Core Design Principle 

The system must feel:
- **Instant** (no waiting)  
- **Effortless** (no booking, no friction)  
- **Human** (not automated-first)  
- **Familiar** (like messaging, not counseling)  
- **Embedded** (inside existing student behavior, not separate)
