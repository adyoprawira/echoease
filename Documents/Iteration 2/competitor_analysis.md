## Competitor Analysis

> We plan three feature areas:
> 1) **Blackboard Support Button**
> 2) **Anonymous Forum**
> 3) **Private Chat**

### 1) Blackboard Support Button

| Competitor | Direct/Indirect | Target Customers | Key Features | Pricing Model | Strengths | Weaknesses |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Our App (UQ Support)** | **Direct** | UQ students | One-click support button embedded natively in the Blackboard sidebar; zero-booking access. | Free | Eliminates "search friction." By living where students study daily, it addresses the 90% preference for Blackboard integration found in our research. | High dependency on university IT infrastructure and Blackboard's sidebar layout limitations. |
| **UQ Counselling** | Indirect | Enrolled students | Web-based contact forms; phone-based triage; office-hour availability. | Free | High clinical trust and professional authority; established brand within the university ecosystem. | High friction; requires leaving the study workflow to search the UQ website; perceived as "too formal" for early-stage stress. |
| **TalkCampus** | Indirect | Global students | Single Sign-On (SSO) for university students via a standalone mobile application. | Institutional Subscription | 24/7 global availability; professional moderation; multi-language support for international students. | Requires a separate app download and login, creating a significant "entry barrier" compared to an embedded button. |

### 2) Anonymous Forum

| Competitor | Direct/Indirect | Target Customers | Key Features | Pricing Model | Strengths | Weaknesses |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Our App (UQ Support)** | **Direct** | UQ students | Anonymous, peer-led discussion boards; focused on specific UQ units/campus life; moderated by students. | Free | High relatability; caters to the 85% of students who prefer "passive consumption" (reading others' stories) to feel validated. | Risk of "echo chambers" or slow response times during off-peak hours if community volume is low. |
| **Togetherall** | Direct | Students & Employees | "Bricks" for creative expression; anonymous moderated community; clinically managed safety. | Institutional Subscription | Extremely safe; clinically moderated 24/7; proven to reduce isolation through shared, creative "bricks." | The interface is less like modern messaging/social media, which can feel dated or less intuitive to younger students. |
| **Reddit (r/uq)** | Indirect | UQ Community | Public subreddit for UQ news and student life; student-run and unofficial. | Free | Massive active user base; unfiltered and highly relatable "real talk" regarding specific courses and tutors. | Not a dedicated wellbeing space; high risk of toxicity, trolling, or unhelpful advice due to lack of professional moderation. |

### 3) Private Chat

| Competitor | Direct/Indirect | Target Customers | Key Features | Pricing Model | Strengths | Weaknesses |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Our App (UQ Support)** | **Direct** | UQ students | Real-time messaging with trained peer mentors; non-clinical "chat" feel; escalation pathway to staff. | Free | Meets the 75% student demand for peer relatability over AI; feels "safe" and low-pressure compared to a formal appointment. | Scalability issues; maintaining "instant" response times requires a large, consistently active pool of student mentors. |
| **Wysa** | Indirect | Students seeking AI tools | AI-driven CBT chatbot; mood tracking; automated therapeutic exercises and mindfulness. | Freemium | Instantaneous 24/7 response; totally private; zero human judgment, which appeals to highly anxious users. | 60% of our interviewed students found automated tools "impersonal" and prefer human connection for emotional validation. |
| **headspace Care** | Direct | Youth (12-25) | Text-based coaching with mental health professionals; 24/7 availability via mobile app. | Subscription / Insurance | High-quality clinical standards; established brand trust; very professional and safe environment. | Can feel "clinical" or "scary" for students experiencing minor academic burnout; long wait times during peak periods. |
